The Hot Hand Under Scrutiny: Bias Correction, Shot Selection, Heterogeneity, and Predictive Validation of Short-Term Dependence in NBA Shooting

Revisits the hot hand debate in basketball with two decades of shot-tracking data, a hand-written Bayesian Gibbs sampler, and hidden Markov models to separate a genuine effect from statistical illusions and shot selection. Most of the apparent hot hand disappears once both are corrected for.

The Architecture of Collective Play: A Multilevel Network Analysis of Passing Structure in European Football with an Embedded Natural Experiment in Spectator Absence

Processes 5.7 million match events into passing networks across four European leagues to test whether they really play in distinct styles, including a natural experiment on matches played without spectators. Leagues turn out to differ more in where on the pitch teams play than in how their networks are structured.

Two Views of British Weather: A Spatial Snapshot and a Century-Long Trend

Combines live weather data from dozens of UK cities with a century and a half of Met Office records to test whether British weather follows real geographic structure and whether the climate has genuinely shifted. The recent warming trend has clearly picked up speed compared with earlier decades.

A Multivariate Statistical Analysis of Music-Listening Profiles and Self-Reported Mental Health

Tests whether music taste is statistically linked to depression, anxiety, or insomnia once age and daily habits are accounted for, cross-checking the strongest result with Bayesian analysis, robust statistics, and a neural network. A taste for rock and metal held up as a genuine link to depression through every test.

Composer Style and Musical Mode in Classical Piano Music Through Hierarchical Statistical Characterisation and Controllable Symbolic Generation

Asks whether a composer's style is a fixed fingerprint or shifts with musical mode, across close to 300 classical piano works, using hierarchical Bayesian models and hidden Markov models alongside LSTMs and a Transformer for generation. Composer identity explains a real share of a piece's structure, but mode barely changes it.

Three Roads to Causality: Estimating the Growth Effect of European Union Accession

Tests whether joining the EU actually causes faster growth using time series diagnostics, three separate causal inference strategies, and a machine learning model built to recover country-level effects. Once selection bias is stripped out, the growth boost from membership is much smaller than it first looks.
